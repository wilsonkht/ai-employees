# Writing the first three seconds

Nothing else in the ad matters if this fails. On paid social you are interrupting someone who did
not ask for you, on a muted phone, mid-scroll.

A hook is three things firing at once, and you write all three every time:

1. **The spoken line** — what's said out loud in the first ~3 seconds
2. **The on-screen text** — what a muted viewer reads (most of them)
3. **The opening visual** — what's happening in frame at 0:00

If you hand over a hook without all three, it isn't a hook, it's a sentence.

## The rules

- **Specific beats clever.** "I found $14,000 in missed deductions" beats "Tired of overpaying?"
  Numbers, names, and objects are concrete. Adjectives aren't.
- **Motion at 0:00.** Nobody's face sitting still. Something moves, changes, or is already in
  progress. Start mid-action, not mid-introduction.
- **Never open with the brand.** The logo is not a hook. Neither is "Hi, I'm —".
- **On-screen text is short.** Six to nine words. It has to be readable at a glance, and it must
  not simply repeat the spoken line word for word — say two things at once.
- **Pattern-match to the feed.** A hook should look native to the platform it's on. A polished
  studio shot reads as an ad in three-tenths of a second.
- **It has to pay off.** A hook that doesn't connect to the body is bait. Hold rate goes up,
  conversions go down, and you've made the account worse.
- **Never invent a number** to make a hook stronger. Pull it from `Offer.md`, `Winners.md`, or
  `Performance/`, or write `[NEEDS: your real figure]`.

## Hook patterns worth having in your hands

| Pattern | Shape | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Confession | "I did the wrong thing for N years" | "I ran my whole company off a shoebox until 2024." |
| Callout | Names the exact viewer | "If you've got more than three trucks and no bookkeeper—" |
| Number drop | Leads with a real figure | "My CPA found $14,000 I'd already lost." |
| Contradiction | Breaks an assumption | "Your CPA is not doing your books. I promise you." |
| Mid-story | Starts at the tense part | "So it's July, and March was never reconciled." |
| Demonstration | Shows before saying | Screen recording of a messy ledger, no talking |
| Negative | Tells them not to buy | "Don't hire a bookkeeper if you're under $500k. Here's why." |
| Question they'd answer | Real, not rhetorical | "When did you last look at your P&L? Actual date." |

## Procedure

1. Read the approved concept — its angle, emotion, and awareness level. The hook has to serve
   *that* argument.
2. Write **ten** internally. Do not stop at four; the good ones come after the obvious ones.
3. Kill anything that could open an ad for any other business in the category. If a competitor
   could run it unchanged, it's generic.
4. Read each one out loud in your head against the clock. Over 3 seconds of speech, cut words.
5. Score the survivors on the table below and hand over the **top four**, ranked.

| Check | Fails if |
|---|---|
| Under 3 seconds spoken | More than ~12 words |
| Works muted | On-screen text alone still lands |
| Concrete | Contains a person, number, object, or date |
| Native | Looks like organic content, not a commercial |
| Honest | Every claim traces to a file |
| Distinct | Uses a different pattern from the other three |

## Worked example

**Concept:** "Your CPA Is Not Your Bookkeeper" (solution-aware · vindication · a bad advisor), for
Ridgeline Books. Objection being killed: "I already have a CPA."

| # | Spoken (0:00–0:03) | On-screen text | Opening visual |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Your CPA is not doing your books. I know you think they are." | YOUR CPA ISN'T DOING THIS | Hand flipping through a stack of receipts on a truck dash |
| 2 | "I paid my accountant for four years to do a job he was never doing." | 4 YEARS. WRONG JOB. | Founder in a shop, walking, mid-sentence |
| 3 | "Here's the exact thing your CPA hands back in April and calls it done." | WHAT APRIL LOOKS LIKE | Screen recording scrolling a chaotic transaction list |
| 4 | "Ask your accountant when your books were last reconciled. Watch the pause." | ASK THEM. WATCH THE PAUSE. | Phone on speaker, ringing, sitting on a workbench |

Ranked: 1, 4, 2, 3. Hook 1 states the contradiction fastest and the on-screen text carries it for
muted viewers. Hook 4 is the one I'm least sure about — it asks the viewer to do work — but it's
the most distinct, so it earns a slot in the test. Note that none of these contain a dollar figure:
`Offer.md` has no documented savings number for Ridgeline, so none gets written into a hook.

## Failure modes

- **Four rewrites of one hook.** Different words, same pattern. Use four different rows from the
  pattern table.
- **The hook that eats the ad.** So dramatic the body can't pay it off. Watch this in confessions.
- **On-screen text that's a subtitle.** If it just transcribes the spoken line, you wasted the
  strongest real estate in the ad.
- **Compliance bait.** Income, health, or "guaranteed" claims in the hook are the fastest way to
  get an account restricted. Flag them; offer a compliant rewrite.
- **No visual direction.** "Founder talks to camera" is not direction. Say where, doing what, and
  what's in frame.

# Writing an ad script

A script is a production document. If the person holding it can't film it on Thursday without
texting you a question, you didn't finish.

Two structures cover almost everything a small business runs on paid social. Pick one and commit —
a script that's half testimonial and half sales letter converts like neither.

## Structure A — UGC testimonial (30–45s)

One person, phone camera, talking like a human who bought the thing. Feels organic. Best for cold
audiences and problem-aware buyers.

| Beat | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0:00–0:03 | From `hook-writing`. Spoken line + on-screen text + motion. |
| The before | 0:03–0:10 | The specific situation they were in. One concrete detail, not a summary. |
| The turn | 0:10–0:16 | How they found it. Casual. "My buddy who runs a plumbing outfit told me—" |
| The mechanism | 0:16–0:28 | What actually happens when you use it. Show it if you can. |
| The proof | 0:28–0:36 | A real result or a real detail. Never a fabricated number. |
| The CTA | 0:36–0:42 | One action, said plainly, once. |

## Structure B — Direct response (45–90s)

Founder or presenter, argument-driven, built to convince rather than to feel authentic. Best for
solution-aware and product-aware audiences, retargeting, and expensive offers.

| Beat | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0:00–0:03 | The claim or contradiction. |
| Callout | 0:03–0:08 | Who this is for, said out loud, so the wrong people leave. |
| Problem, sharpened | 0:08–0:20 | Not "bookkeeping is hard" — the exact moment it hurts. |
| Why the usual fix fails | 0:20–0:32 | The enemy from the concept. This is where belief shifts. |
| The mechanism | 0:32–0:50 | How yours works, in steps, concretely. |
| Proof | 0:50–1:05 | Documented results, named customers, or a demonstration. Sourced. |
| Objection + risk reversal | 1:05–1:15 | The guarantee or the thing that makes trying it cheap. |
| CTA | 1:15–1:25 | One action. Repeated once at most. |

## Rules for the words

- **Write for the ear.** Short sentences. Contractions. Read it aloud; if you run out of breath,
  cut it.
- **One idea per sentence.** Two ideas in a sentence means the viewer keeps neither.
- **Their vocabulary.** Pull nouns from `Audience.md`. Contractors say "jobs" and "trucks," not
  "service delivery units."
- **Show, don't assert.** "Everything's reconciled by the 10th" is a claim. A screen recording of
  the close is proof.
- **One CTA.** Never "click the link, or DM us, or check the bio." Pick the one that matches the
  campaign objective.
- **Every claim gets sourced.** Cite `Offer.md` or `Winners.md` in a comment beside the line. Any
  number you can't source becomes `[NEEDS: …]` — never a plausible-sounding placeholder.
- **Pace the platform.** A visual change every 2–3 seconds for TikTok and Reels; a little slower
  for Facebook feed. Note it in the shot column.

## The deliverable

Every script ships with: runtime estimate, aspect ratio, a numbered shot list, on-screen text
called out separately, and a B-roll list.

## Worked example

Ridgeline Books, done-for-you bookkeeping for HVAC and plumbing contractors, $650/mo, "books closed
by the 10th or the month is free." Concept 2 from `_memory/Log/Concepts-2026-07-27.md` — "Your CPA
Is Not Your Bookkeeper" (solution-aware · vindication · a bad advisor) — with hook #1 from
`hook-writing`. Structure A, because the audience is cold and a contractor in a real truck
out-performs a studio look in this category.

```markdown
# Script — Concept 2: Your CPA Is Not Your Bookkeeper
Format: UGC, 9:16, ~40s · Talent: owner-operator, filmed in a work truck or shop
Concept: _memory/Log/Concepts-2026-07-27.md · Hook: #1

| # | Time | Spoken | On-screen text | Visual |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0:00 | "Your CPA is not doing your books. I know you think they are." | YOUR CPA ISN'T DOING THIS | Hand flipping receipts on a truck dash, engine noise |
| 2 | 0:04 | "Mine wasn't. Four years. I found out in July when I went for a line of credit." | | Walking into a shop, phone-in-hand, natural light |
| 3 | 0:11 | "The bank wanted current financials. What I had was a shoebox and a guy who files taxes in April." | THE BANK ASKED. I HAD NOTHING. | Cut to a genuinely messy pile of receipts on a desk |
| 4 | 0:19 | "So now somebody actually does it every month. Books closed by the 10th — that's the whole deal." | CLOSED BY THE 10TH | Screen recording: reconciled ledger scrolling, month-end view |
| 5 | 0:28 | "If they miss the 10th, I don't pay for that month." | MISS IT = FREE MONTH | Back to talent, half-shrug, still walking |
| 6 | 0:34 | "Ridgeline Books. Link's right there. Takes ten minutes to hand it off." | | Product shot, then CTA card |

**B-roll needed:** receipts on dash · shop walk-in · messy desk pile · screen recording of a
closed month · truck exterior
**Sourcing:** "closed by the 10th or the month is free" — `_memory/Offer.md`, line 12. Shot 3's
line-of-credit story is the founder's own, from onboarding — confirm before filming.
**Do not add:** a savings figure. Nothing in `_memory/` documents one.
```

## Variants

When they ask for variants of a working script, change **one** thing at a time and label it:
hook-only variants, CTA variants, proof-block variants, or length cuts (a 40s cut down to 15s for
Reels). Mixing variables makes the result unreadable — see `creative-testing`.

## Failure modes

- **Unshootable direction.** "Cinematic drone shot of the city at dawn" for a plumbing company
  with an iPhone. Write to the gear they have.
- **The essay.** Beautiful copy with no shot direction. An editor can't film prose.
- **Testimonial fabrication.** Never write words into a customer's mouth as if they said them.
  Mark UGC talent lines as *scripted for talent*, and if the ad implies a real customer experience
  and you're inventing it, say so and let the user decide.
- **Two CTAs.** Cuts response every time.
- **Runtime drift.** 140 words is not a 30-second ad. Roughly 2.5 words per second spoken — count.

# Tearing down a competitor's ad

The point is not admiration. The point is extracting the reusable part — the angle, the structure,
the objection it kills — and leaving the parts that only work because it's their brand.

You can only tear down what the user gives you. Ads they saved in `Swipe/`, a pasted transcript, a
screenshot, a description they typed. **You do not have access to their competitors' performance
data, and you never estimate it.** "This ad has been running a long time" is something the user
tells you from an ad library, not something you infer.

## What you need before you start

| Input | Why it matters | If missing |
|---|---|---|
| The creative itself (video, screenshot, transcript) | Everything below | Ask for it; don't work from a brand name |
| How long it's been running | The only real signal that it works | Note "run time unknown" and say the read is structural only |
| The landing page or offer | Half the ad's job is offer, not creative | Say so; your read stops at the creative |
| Which competitor and where they saw it | Context, and which platform's norms apply | Ask |

## The teardown

Work through these in order. Skip nothing, but keep each to a couple of lines.

1. **First three seconds.** Transcribe the spoken line, the on-screen text, and describe the
   opening frame. Which pattern is it? (see `hook-writing`)
2. **The angle.** Awareness level, emotion, and enemy — the same three dials from
   `creative-angles`. Name them explicitly.
3. **The structure.** Map it to beats with rough timestamps. UGC? Direct response? Something else?
4. **The offer mechanics.** Price shown or hidden, guarantee, urgency, risk reversal, the CTA and
   where it goes.
5. **The proof.** What are they using to be believed — testimonials, demonstration, numbers,
   authority, volume of reviews?
6. **Production reality.** Phone or crew? Could the user make this with what they own? Say so
   plainly, because it decides whether any of this is actionable.
7. **The objection it kills.** Usually one, sometimes two. This is the most portable part.

## Then the judgment

Three lists. Short.

- **Steal** — the structural moves that would work for the user's offer. Angles and structures are
  fair game; they're not ownable.
- **Skip** — what only works because of their brand, budget, celebrity, or category.
- **Beat** — where the ad is weak and what the user could do that this competitor can't. Usually
  specificity, proof, or a niche they're too big to speak to.

Never copy their words. Copy the argument, then write it in the user's voice from `Audience.md`.

## Worked example

**Ad:** Ledgerly (national bookkeeping SaaS), 9:16, ~35s, saved in `Swipe/ledgerly-july-2026.mp4`.
User says it's been in Meta's ad library since April.

- **First 3s.** Spoken: "Small business owners are overpaying their accountants by thousands."
  On-screen: OVERPAYING? Opens on a stack of invoices being dropped on a desk. Pattern: number
  drop + callout, with a claim doing the heavy lifting.
- **Angle.** Problem-aware · frustration · a bad advisor (the expensive accountant).
- **Structure.** Direct response. Hook 0:00, callout 0:04, problem 0:07, "why the usual fix fails"
  0:14, product demo 0:20, pricing 0:28, CTA 0:32. Textbook.
- **Offer mechanics.** Price shown ($199/mo), free trial, no guarantee, CTA to a signup page.
- **Proof.** Product screen recording plus "trusted by 12,000 businesses." No named customer, no
  documented result.
- **Production.** Studio-lit presenter, motion graphics. The user cannot match this, and shouldn't
  try — a real contractor in a real truck out-performs this look in this category.
- **Objection killed.** "I already pay someone for this."

**Steal:** the "why the usual fix fails" beat at 0:14 — it's doing all the persuasion work, and
Ridgeline's "Your CPA Is Not Your Bookkeeper" concept is a sharper version of the same move.
**Skip:** the studio look, the 12,000-businesses proof (Ridgeline can't claim scale and shouldn't
try), and leading with price.
**Beat:** they're generic — "small business owners." Ridgeline can say "if you run more than three
trucks" in the first two seconds and take the whole HVAC and plumbing slice of their audience.
Ledgerly also has no guarantee; "closed by the 10th or the month is free" (`_memory/Offer.md`) is
a harder promise than anything in this ad.

## Where it goes

Write the teardown to `_memory/Log/Teardown-<competitor>-YYYY-MM-DD.md`. If the teardown reveals
something durable about the category — an objection everyone is fighting, a claim everyone makes —
add a line to `_memory/Audience.md` or `_memory/Offer.md` so it informs future concepts.

## Failure modes

- **Assuming it works.** A bad ad can run for months at a company that isn't watching. Without run
  time or a statement from the user, say the read is structural.
- **Inventing their metrics.** You have no CPA, no CTR, and no spend for anyone else's ad. Ever.
- **Copying language.** Lifting phrasing produces a worse ad and a legal question. Take the
  argument, not the sentences.
- **Teardown with no output.** Every teardown ends in at least one concept the user could run.
  Otherwise it's a book report.
- **Only tearing down giants.** The most useful teardowns are of competitors the same size, whose
  production the user can actually match.
